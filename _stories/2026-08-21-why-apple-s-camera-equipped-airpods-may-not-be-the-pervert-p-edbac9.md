# Why Apple’s camera-equipped AirPods may not be the ‘pervert pods’ consumers fear

[Sarah Perez](https://techcrunch.com/author/sarah-perez/)

9:19 AM PDT · August 18, 2026

[](https://www.facebook.com/sharer.php?u=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F08%2F18%2Fwhy-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear%2F)[](https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?url=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F08%2F18%2Fwhy-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear%2F&text=Why+Apple%E2%80%99s+camera-equipped+AirPods+may+not+be+the+%E2%80%98pervert+pods%E2%80%99+consumers+fear&via=techcrunch)[](https://www.linkedin.com/shareArticle?url=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F08%2F18%2Fwhy-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear%2F&title=Why+Apple%E2%80%99s+camera-equipped+AirPods+may+not+be+the+%E2%80%98pervert+pods%E2%80%99+consumers+fear&summary=Apple%E2%80%99s+leaked+camera-equipped+AirPods+might+avoid+the+privacy+pitfalls+of+other+AI+wearables+by+preventing+users+from+recording+photos+and+videos.&mini=1&source=TechCrunch)[](https://www.reddit.com/submit?url=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F08%2F18%2Fwhy-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear%2F&title=Why+Apple%E2%80%99s+camera-equipped+AirPods+may+not+be+the+%E2%80%98pervert+pods%E2%80%99+consumers+fear)[](mailto:?subject=Why+Apple%E2%80%99s+camera-equipped+AirPods+may+not+be+the+%E2%80%98pervert+pods%E2%80%99+consumers+fear&body=Article%3A+https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F08%2F18%2Fwhy-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear%2F)[](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/why-apples-camera-equipped-airpods-may-not-be-the-pervert-pods-consumers-fear/)

Camera-equipped AI wearables today have developed a bit of a reputation problem. Devices like Meta’s Ray-Bans raise concerns about people being recorded without their consent. So the idea that Apple is reportedly preparing to release AirPods with cameras in them seems to conflict with the iPhone maker’s desire to position itself as a [privacy-focused](https://www.apple.com/privacy/) tech company.

But Apple’s now-leaked camera-equipped AirPods may not be as creepy as they first sound.

The rumored cameras were reportedly [confirmed via video footage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/apples-new-macos-update-reportedly-contains-a-video-of-airpods-with-a-camera/) found in Apple’s macOS 26.7 RC (short for “release candidate,” or the final test version of the software before it ships publicly). The footage, found by researcher [Aaron Perris](https://aaronperris.com/), shows a man holding up a book while wearing AirPods and apparently having a conversation with Siri about it.

The audio track accompanying the video says: “With Visual Intelligence, your world becomes savable. See something you like? Just ask me to save it for later.”

The addition of cameras to the popular wireless earbuds is further confirmed through code that references a [“Hair Detected”](https://x.com/aaronp613/status/2089698085823734171) error, which would warn users when their hair is covering the AirPods’ camera. The launch of such a device could very easily backfire on Apple if not done right.

>   
> If you have long hair that blocks the camera, there will be an error that is titled "Hair Detected" that says "to get the most accurate information about things in your environment, make sure AirPods… [https://t.co/hUbWovpsyc](https://t.co/hUbWovpsyc)
> 
> — Aaron (@aaronp613) [August 18, 2026](https://x.com/aaronp613/status/2089698085823734171?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

For a company whose brand is built on trust, this is a bigger gamble for Apple than one of its typical hardware refreshes. AirPods currently blend as a simple iPhone accessory that people wear all day without a second thought. A change like this could instead push them to the front of people’s minds, making them wonder whether the person opposite them is secretly recording.

Apple appears to have thought this through, based on prior reporting. According to [an earlier report from Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-07/apple-s-camera-equipped-airpods-reach-advanced-testing-stage-in-ai-device-push), the AirPods with cameras won’t be able to record media. As Gurman put it:

> The cameras essentially act as eyes for the Siri digital assistant and aren’t designed to take photos or video. These components — located in both the right and left earbuds — allow the device to capture visual information in low resolution.

In other words, the goal isn’t to turn one of Apple’s best-selling accessories into a surveillance device (lest they become “pervert pods”). It’s to provide a way for AirPods owners to engage with the newly [upgraded Siri AI,](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/apples-long-awaited-ai-siri-overhaul-is-finally-here/) which is set to ship in September with [Apple’s iOS 27](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/apple-opens-its-new-siri-ai-to-everyone-with-the-ios-27-public-beta/) and other software updates.

That means an AirPods wearer could look at something — like a book, as shown in the leaked video, or perhaps some ingredients they’re preparing to cook, per Gurman’s earlier reporting — and simply ask Siri for help. The feature could also be useful for turn-by-turn walking directions when navigating an unfamiliar city.

For Apple, the bigger strategic bet is that this feature could also push consumers toward, if not a screen-free future entirely, then at least one where there’s less need to pull out an iPhone throughout the day. By baking AI access into a commonly worn and already socially acceptable wearable, Apple’s customers could interact with the world around them in a more natural way than staring down at a glowing screen.

There is one wrinkle, though. Gurman also reported that the new AirPods would include an LED indicator that lights up whenever the device is sharing visual data to the cloud. He questioned the light’s visibility, given the AirPods’ small size, but either way, its inclusion could work against Apple. Though it’s the right thing to do from a transparency and privacy perspective, that LED risks visually lumping AirPods in with other AI wearables already facing public skepticism, like the [Meta Ray-Bans](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/meta-launches-two-new-ray-ban-glasses-designed-for-prescription-wearers/), [Snap’s Specs](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/snap-ceo-sidesteps-specs-pre-order-questions-on-q2-earnings-call/), or [Google’s new AI glasses](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-xr-io-2026/). Apple can control what the cameras do, but it’ll have far less control over what people assume they do.
