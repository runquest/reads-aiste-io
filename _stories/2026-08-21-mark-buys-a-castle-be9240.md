# Mark buys a castle

Mark Zuckerberg just bought a cozy abode close to Meta’s international headquarters in Ireland. By cozy abode, [we mean a castle](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/20/mark-zuckerberg-buys-440-acre-strancally-castle-estate-in-ireland). Zuck bought the 19th-century Strancally Castle and its 440-acre grounds, an estate estimated to have cost him anywhere from $23 million to $35 million.

“Mark and his family are excited to continue caring for this historic home and look forward to spending time in Ireland, where Meta maintains its international headquarters,” a spokesperson told the press about the purchase.

We’ve reached out to Meta for further comment.  

The home is stunning: It sits on a river, surrounded by greenery, and the Irish Times [reported in 2001](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2026/08/20/mark-zuckerbergs-irish-castle-is-immortalised-in-verse-as-the-site-of-gruesome-deeds/) that it has at least 11 bedrooms, four tower suites, a library, a dining room, and a drawing room. Zuck bought the castle from a financier who’d lived there with his family for 25 years. 

Meta’s international headquarters is in Dublin, where the company opened a [31,000-square-foot data center](https://www.metacareers.com/blog/facebook-clonee-data-center-ireland/) in 2017, just 20 minutes from the city. Waterford, where Zuck’s new castle is, is about a two-hour drive from Dublin.

This isn’t Zuck’s first big real estate purchase this year. He and his wife also reportedly [splashed out $170 million](https://people.com/mark-zuckerberg-buys-usd170m-mansion-on-billionaire-bunker-island-in-miami-11918467) for a home in the same exclusive Miami neighborhood as Jeff Bezos, joining a slew of tech billionaires leaving California as the state seeks to implement a form of wealth tax.
